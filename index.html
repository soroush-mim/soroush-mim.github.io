$ cat about.md

An agent that acts in the world needs an internal model of it: what is where, how things move, and what is likely to happen next. Video and 3D models have become good at short-horizon prediction, but many of them lose the scene once it falls out of their context window, and they get brittle when the input drifts away from the training data. I want to build world models that are grounded in 3D geometry, keep a persistent memory of the scene within a fixed budget, and adapt as conditions change.

My recent work covers two of these pieces. In Evict3R, I looked at streaming 3D reconstruction transformers, whose key-value cache grows with every frame. A training-free policy that evicts redundant tokens and keeps the informative ones roughly halves peak memory on 7-Scenes (18.63 GB to 9.39 GB), while accuracy and completeness drop by only 0.003. In MODE-TTA, with collaborators at the University of Sydney, we adapt 3D vision-language models at test time by modelling each class as a Gaussian mixture that is updated online as data arrives. Before that, my M.Sc. thesis at Amirkabir University of Technology (GPA 4.0/4.0), supervised by Maryam Amirmazlaghani, was on adversarial robustness and led to MemLoss.

I’m a research assistant at the Autonomous & Intelligent Systems Lab at AUT, working with Mahdi Javanmardi, and I collaborate remotely with Ali Cheraghian’s group at the University of Sydney. I’m based in Tehran and looking for a PhD position in world models, 3D vision, or embodied AI. If our interests overlap, I’d be glad to hear from you.

Each new frame adds tokens to the cache. Lower the budget and the least-attended tokens get evicted (dim cells). Most of the attention mass survives, because attention is sparse.
